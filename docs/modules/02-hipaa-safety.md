### Topic 1: The DWIHN Approved AI Tool Catalog in Detail (Module 2)

#### Detailed Definition and Introduction
Not all AI tools are created equal. The AI ecosystem is split broadly into two categories: **Consumer AI** and **Enterprise AI**. The DWIHN Approved Tool Catalog is a curated list of strictly vetted Enterprise AI tools that are legally and technically safe for DWIHN staff to use.

#### Fundamentals and Prerequisite Concepts
* **Consumer AI (e.g., free ChatGPT, free Claude, web Gemini):** These tools are free because *you* and your data are the product. When you type a prompt into a Consumer AI, your text is stored, analyzed, and used to train future versions of the AI. 
* **Enterprise AI (e.g., Lumenore Impact, secure MS Copilot, Genesys AI):** These are paid, heavily secured tools protected by a BAA (Business Associate Agreement). The vendor legally guarantees that your data is encrypted, segregated, and **never** used to train the global AI model. When you close the window, your data is forgotten.

#### Why the Concept is Needed & What Problem it Solves
Using Consumer AI creates a massive, silent compliance risk. If a staff member pastes a case history into free ChatGPT to check for grammar, that member's PHI (Protected Health Information) is now living on external servers, violating HIPAA.

#### Step-by-Step Process: Navigating the Catalog
1. Navigate to the DWIHN Intranet homepage.
2. Click on the "IT Security & Compliance" tab.
3. Select "Approved AI Tools."
4. Review the matrix: It lists the Tool Name, the Approved Use Case (e.g., Data Analytics, Call Center), and the Data Tier allowed.

#### Common Mistakes and Misconceptions
* **Misconception:** "I didn't include the member's last name, so it's fine to use the free tool."
* **Correction:** PHI includes 18 identifiers, including zip codes, dates of service, and unique medical conditions. Partial anonymization by a human is highly prone to error. Only use approved tools.

### Topic 2: HIPAA Boundaries and Broken Rules (Module 2)

#### Detailed Explanation of the Concept
The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) applies to AI exactly as it applies to email, faxes, or physical filing cabinets. An AI vendor must sign a BAA for the tool to be HIPAA-compliant. If no BAA exists, no PHI can enter the tool.

#### What Happens When Rules Are Broken?
* **Data Leakage:** Once PHI enters a public LLM, it is mathematically woven into the neural network. It is nearly impossible to extract or delete it.
* **Organizational Impact:** Severe financial penalties for DWIHN, loss of community trust, and potential state/federal audits.
* **Individual Impact:** Disciplinary action up to and including termination, and potential loss of professional licensure.

#### Troubleshooting Scenarios
* **Scenario:** A colleague sends you a summary generated by an unapproved public AI that contains member data.
* **Action:** Do not forward it. Delete the document, inform the colleague immediately to stop using the unapproved tool, and follow the 5-Step Incident Reporting process (which we cover in Session 11).

### Topic 3: The Four Data Classification Tiers & The Wallet Analogy (Module 3)

#### Detailed Definition and Introduction
To know which tool to use, you must first know what kind of data you are holding. DWIHN uses a four-tier data classification model: Public, Internal, Confidential, and Restricted.

#### How it Works Internally (The Wallet Analogy)
To memorize these tiers instantly, think of the contents of your physical wallet:

1. **Public (Tier 1):** *Your Business Card.* 
   * **Definition:** Information meant for the world. 
   * **Example:** DWIHN's address, the public hotline number, a press release about a new community initiative.
   * **AI Rule:** Can be used in almost any AI tool, but stick to approved tools for work tasks.

2. **Internal (Tier 2):** *Your Driver’s License.*
   * **Definition:** You show it to verify who you are, but you put it back in your pocket. It's for DWIHN employees only.
   * **Example:** The cafeteria menu, the staff directory, generalized operational memos.
   * **AI Rule:** Must only be used in DWIHN-approved Internal AI tools.

3. **Confidential (Tier 3):** *Your Credit Card.*
   * **Definition:** High risk. You only hand this over to trusted, secure systems (like a bank or a secure payment portal). If stolen, it causes severe financial/reputational damage.
   * **Example:** Unreleased budget reports, strategic plans, employee salary bands, aggregated (but non-PHI) clinical performance data.
   * **AI Rule:** Strictly limited to approved Enterprise AI tools with specific security access (e.g., Lumenore).

4. **Restricted (Tier 4):** *Your Social Security Number & PIN.*
   * **Definition:** The highest level of restriction. Governed by federal law (HIPAA, 42 CFR Part 2).
   * **Example:** Any Protected Health Information (PHI)—member names, diagnoses, treatment plans, Medicaid ID numbers.
   * **AI Rule:** **STOP.** Restricted data can ONLY be processed in specifically designated, HIPAA-cleared, BAA-backed systems.

#### Comparison with Related Concepts
This replaces the old "Confidential vs. Non-Confidential" binary model, providing a nuanced approach that allows staff to use AI flexibly for Tier 1 and 2, while locking down Tiers 3 and 4.

### Topic 4: Decision Scenarios & Desk Card Walkthrough (Module 3)

#### Components and Architecture
Every staff member is provided with a physical "Quick-Reference Desk Card" and a digital version pinned in MS Teams. This card features a decision tree: 
* What data tier am I holding? → Which tool am I allowed to open?

#### Practical Implementation & Real-World Scenarios
Let's practice applying the Wallet Analogy.

* **Scenario A:** You are a Care Coordinator. You need to write an email reminding staff about the new parking policy. 
  * *Analysis:* Parking policy is Internal (Tier 2 / Driver's License). You can safely use the approved Enterprise Copilot to draft this email.
* **Scenario B:** You are a Program Manager. You download a spreadsheet containing the names and addresses of 50 members who missed appointments. You want to ask AI to format the Excel sheet.
  * *Analysis:* Names and addresses are PHI. This is Restricted (Tier 4 / SSN). You absolutely **cannot** use a standard AI tool to format this. You must use the designated HIPAA-cleared analytics tool or format it manually.

#### Best Practices
When in doubt, elevate the classification. If you aren't sure if something is Internal or Confidential, treat it as Confidential.

### Key Takeaways (Important Points to Remember)
1. **Consumer vs. Enterprise:** Never use free, consumer AI tools for DWIHN business.
2. **The Catalog:** Always check the DWIHN Approved Tool Catalog before using a new AI feature.
3. **The Wallet Analogy:** Public (Business Card), Internal (Driver's License), Confidential (Credit Card), Restricted (SSN).
4. **Protect PHI:** Restricted data demands the highest level of scrutiny. When in doubt, do not prompt.

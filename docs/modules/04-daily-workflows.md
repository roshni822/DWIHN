### Topic 1: Role-Specific Workflows

#### Detailed Definition and Introduction
AI is not a one-size-fits-all tool. A Care Coordinator’s use of AI looks vastly different from a Compliance Officer’s. Today, we break down "A Day in the Life" for each major DWIHN role.

#### Practical Implementation & Industry Scenarios

**1. The Care Coordinator Workflow**
* **Problem it solves:** Drowning in paperwork and disjointed communication.
* **9:00 AM Task:** Reviewing a massive case file transfer from a hospital. 
  * *Workflow:* (Using an approved internal tool), the Coordinator asks the AI to extract only the medications and recent behavioral incidents into a bulleted list. 
* **2:00 PM Task:** Explaining a complex care plan to a non-English speaking family.
  * *Workflow:* Using AI to translate the care plan summary into Spanish at a 5th-grade reading level, ensuring the family understands their requirements.

**2. The Program Manager Workflow**
* **Problem it solves:** Bottlenecks in team communication and reporting.
* **10:00 AM Task:** Aggregating updates from 5 different direct reports into a single weekly update for the Director.
  * *Workflow:* The manager pastes the 5 rough emails into the AI and prompts: "Synthesize these 5 updates into a single, cohesive executive summary table."
* **3:00 PM Task:** Drafting performance review outlines.
  * *Workflow:* Using AI to create a template for a performance review conversation focusing on empathy and growth.

**3. The Admin & Operations Workflow**
* **Problem it solves:** Repetitive, manual data formatting and scheduling.
* **11:00 AM Task:** Formatting a messy list of 200 community provider names and messy addresses.
  * *Workflow:* Prompting the AI to "Extract the street names, cities, and zip codes from this text and format it as a CSV table."

**4. The Compliance Officer Workflow**
* **1:00 PM Task:** Comparing a new state regulation document against a DWIHN internal policy document to find gaps.
  * *Workflow:* Uploading both (in an approved tool) and asking the AI: "Identify any discrepancies between Document A and Document B regarding incident reporting timelines."

### Topic 2: Writing Emails, Summaries, and Reports with AI Assistance

#### Fundamentals and Prerequisite Concepts
The concept here is **"The Blank Page Problem."** Staring at a blank screen is the most time-consuming part of writing. AI's greatest strength is generating the "shitty first draft" (SFD) instantly.

#### Step-by-Step Working/Process
1. **Braindump:** Type a completely unstructured, grammatically terrible thought into the AI. (e.g., "Tell vendor they are late on invoice #123, need it by Friday, or we pause contract. Be nice but firm.")
2. **Apply Anatomy:** Wrap that braindump in a 5-part prompt.
3. **Generate:** Let the AI write the draft.

#### Comparison with Related Concepts
* **Manual Writing:** Takes 15 minutes. High cognitive load.
* **AI Writing:** Takes 2 minutes. Low cognitive load. Shifts your brain from "Creator" to "Editor."

### Topic 3: Reviewing and Editing AI Output (Human-in-the-Loop)

#### Detailed Explanation of the Concept
"Human-in-the-Loop" (HITL) is a mandatory DWIHN policy. You must never copy-paste AI output directly into an email or official document without reading every single word. 

#### Why the Concept is Needed
AI can hallucinate dates, invent policies that sound real, or use a tone that is too aggressive or too robotic. If you send an AI-generated email that contains a hallucinated deadline, *you* are responsible for that error, not the AI.

#### Best Practices: The 80/20 Rule
* Let the AI do the 80% heavy lifting (structure, grammar, formatting).
* You do the 20% critical polish (fact-checking, adding human empathy, verifying dates).
* **Pro-tip:** Read the AI output out loud. If it sounds like a robot wrote it, change the adjectives. Remove words like "Furthermore," "Delve," and "Tapestry," which are classic AI hallmarks.

### Topic 4: The Daily AI Checklist — Building Safe Habits

#### Components and Architecture
To ensure safety every single time you use AI, you must mentally run through the **Daily AI Checklist** before pressing 'Enter' on a prompt.

1. **Tool Check:** Am I using a DWIHN-approved Enterprise tool?
2. **Data Check:** Did I apply the Wallet Analogy? Is there any Restricted PHI in my prompt?
3. **Prompt Check:** Did I use the 5-Part Anatomy (Role, Task, Context, Format, Constraint)?
4. **Review Check:** Have I committed to reading the output before I use it?

#### Common Mistakes and Misconceptions
* **Mistake:** Skipping the checklist because you are in a rush. Rushing is when HIPAA violations occur. The checklist takes 3 seconds of mental processing.
